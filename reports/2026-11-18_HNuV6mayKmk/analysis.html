1. Thesis and stance

Ben argues both camps have been disappointed for about eight months: bears wanted BTC under $60K (briefly reached in the summer) and bulls wanted it above $80K (briefly reached in Q3). He wants to show how he sees the market, and says viewers need not agree. His central claim is that several reliable “reset” signals have not yet fired, so a bullish bias would be premature, even though he says he wouldn't mind flipping bullish soon, especially in the pre-halving year.

He frames the next 7–8 weeks as decisive: if the weakness does not occur in that window it becomes less likely for a while, and he expects many bears to become bulls again. He admits there are exceptions to the midterm-year pattern.

5. His reasoning, in order

  • Measure Bitcoin against something other than USD. USD is distorted by money printing, so he uses BTC/USDT dominance and BTC/gold. These ratios point to a reset that has not yet happened.
  • Stablecoin liquidity is leaving. Stablecoin market cap lost both prior lows while BTC's own lows were about the same in February and July.
  • Rate-hike analogue. After the first hike (Sept 2026) BTC followed March 2022 almost exactly: rally, bulls celebrate, then drop. He expects the first ~90 days to rhyme, but not the long grind of 2022–23 because this hiking cycle should be smaller.
  • Equities lead the year-end drop. He had said BTC's year-end weakness is often driven by an equity correction. Nasdaq's YTD path resembles 2018 (early-year correction, then another into year-end); in 2018 many priced-in hikes were later priced out after the equity correction.
  • Proxies are cracking. Coinbase and stablecoin supply broke their summer lows; MicroStrategy shows the pattern he has seen “a thousand times” (blow-off top, two counter-trend rallies, lower high, bleed-out).
  • Commodities first, Bitcoin later. Gold and silver show the same three-low pattern. In 2022 gold bottomed ~1.5 months before BTC, in 2018 ~4 months before, and in 2008 gold bottomed in October with stocks in March 2009. Gold keeps falling because rate-hike expectations keep rising.
  • Dollar strength drains liquidity. DXY should rise to 105–106; Trump's second-term DXY path resembles his first. Rising DXY has historically meant weak BTC in a midterm year.
  • Seasonality. The average BTC path after a September midterm-year first hike is flat for ~1.5 months then down into Q4. He draws a possible path between FTX and 2018.
  • Government-shutdown pattern. Average BTC drop ~20% around shutdowns; he lists each of the last seven (2013: capitulation right after; 2018 three times: sell-offs into them; 2025: less bearish; two in early 2026: sell-offs).
  • On-chain has not reset. He cannot understand why other on-chain analysts are bullish (maybe ETFs?) and hopes to learn at the conference.

12. My interpretation (not Ben's words)

  • His view is a checklist, not a price target. He names reset conditions (MVRV-Z below zero, price below realized price, on-chain risk below 0.1, gold bottoming, stablecoin cap stabilising), so the stance flips when those fire, not at a given price.
  • The scenarios are asymmetric. Either deep reset (~$54K to ~$38K) or a 4th retest of $60K; he treats a quick V-shaped recovery as the least likely.
  • Dependence on gold. The BTC/gold ratio target moves one-for-one with gold, so the $38K figure is only valid if gold stays near $3,783.
  • He may be anchoring on the four-year cycle while arguing against it. He says a deeper Q4 reset would make next year's rally bigger, which makes lower prices “good news” for his longer-term view.
