Bitcoin Drops Below $60k

Benjamin Cowen · published 20 Nov 2026 · watch · analysis of the full transcript

At a glance

  • Thesis: BTC at ~$60K (briefly below it while he recorded) is doing what it normally does in Q4 of a midterm year. He says it is not that crazy, only feels different.
  • Checklist of 'normal': Q4 midterm weakness, drop into a government shutdown, drop after the first hike of a hiking cycle, drop after a September midterm-year hike, and a trip to the realized price (just under $54K). In his words BTC is doing exactly what it historically does.
  • Not yet a bottom by his metrics: on-chain risk ~0.175 (lows come below 0.1) and MVRV Z-score reading (transcribed '27', probably 0.27) versus lows below zero. The realized price has not been breached.
  • Range of outcomes: low 50s would be normal; 30-40K 'still within historical standards' (not a call). Gold, silver, Nasdaq, S&P and stablecoin cap are all below their summer lows, so 'why would Bitcoin not?'.
  • Housekeeping: first Into the Cryptoverse (ITC) conference runs 20-22 Nov; many talks free the week of 7 Dec.

1. Thesis and stance

Ben argues the Q4 dump is the expected outcome, not an anomaly: BTC is 'allergic' to Q4 of midterm years. February, summer and Q4 tend to host lows, give or take a month, and he stresses it is not an exact science. He warns against ignoring base rates ('this time is different' would require every earlier capitulation low to have been different). Tone versus 19 Nov (Anthropic IPO video) is the same bearish-to-neutral near-term line, but with more emphasis on how long bear markets can run and on on-chain levels that have not reset. He says BTC is 'tempting to be bullish' on but he wants to see metals stop falling first.

4. Indicators and metrics

  • On-chain composite risk: ~0.175; prior capitulation lows came below 0.1. Not there yet. He cautions that on-chain data can be spun bullish in any condition.
  • MVRV Z-score: lows tend to be below zero; reading transcribed '27' (doubtful; likely 0.27). Not below zero.
  • Realized / balance price: see levels. Realized not yet breached.
  • % of supply in profit vs loss: the two lines crossed in summer and again now; he says the same double cross happened in 2022 and in 2014.
  • Liquidity risk metric: liquidity has been 'scarce' since 2022, which matches BTC's underperformance. BTC outperforms again only when liquidity risk falls, which can start with a deep equity correction that removes expected rate hikes. No chart possible (no Yahoo series).
  • ETF flows around IPOs: new chart on his site; SpaceX-era ETF flows bottomed ~20 days after the IPO.
  • Not used: RSI, MACD, head-and-shoulders (win rate not better than a coin flip in his view). He prefers momentum (winners keep winning, don't sell winners to buy losers).

5. Reasoning chains

  • Midterm-year Q4 is historically weak -> a drop now is the base rate, so 'dunking' on that view earlier in the year was premature.
  • Hawkish Fed -> tighter policy is the main reason BTC is losing. In Dec 2018 peak hawkishness (two projected 2019 hikes) was priced into the Dec FOMC and BTC sold into it; he sees the same feeling now.
  • Every other asset is lower-lowing (gold, silver, Nasdaq, S&P, Netflix, stablecoin cap) -> BTC making a lower low is consistent, not surprising.
  • On-chain risk and Z-score have not reached historical capitulation zones -> bottom not confirmed.
  • Everyone is now discussing the IPO-cycle idea 'because it played out' -> when a trade is crowded he moves to the next lens, which is the shutdown.

6. Macro and cross-market context

Nasdaq and S&P are below their summer lows; gold at a Q4 low after Q1 and summer lows; silver also lower; Netflix repeats the Feb/summer/Q4 low pattern; stablecoin cap breaking down (Q4 low unknown). Money supply is at record highs but liquidity risk is high. Rate-hike expectations: hiking cycle ongoing; he says BTC tracks the average path after the first hike.

7. Historical comparisons (analogues, not forecasts)

  • 2014-15: bleed into mid-January; sweep of prior high then capitulation. 2018: realized price breached 19 Nov, low in December; Nasdaq -23% in Q4 2018. 2022: gold bottomed late Sep, BTC Nov. 2019-20: apathetic top, sweep of high then pandemic-driven low (he says the pandemic is a poor comparison but the shape fits).
  • Government shutdowns: BTC on average drops into them; he walks through 2013, 2018 (x2), 2025 and 2026 instances (one early-2018 instance 'very similar').

8. If-then conditions

  • If metals keep falling -> hard to call a crypto bottom.
  • If BTC loses 60K -> consistent with every other asset making lower lows (he asks why not).
  • If liquidity risk falls (e.g. via an equity correction removing hikes) -> BTC can outperform again.
  • If realized price is never reached -> would hint this time really is different (said as a possibility).

9. Doubts, hedges, caveats

He does not want to put a balance-price number in people's heads; admits indicators sometimes fail; 'not an exact science'; says no one is right about everything, 'especially me'. He notes BTC may be bottoming as he records.

12. My interpretation (inference, not Ben's words)

  • His method is base-rate stacking: five separate 'normal' conditions all point to weakness, but they are correlated (all are midterm-year/hiking-cycle effects), so they are not independent evidence.
  • He leans on metals as a lead indicator while saying it is an odd idea; it gives a falsifiable trigger (gold/silver stabilising).
  • The framing is calibrated toward not selling at the lows: the same data that says downside is normal is used to argue against panic selling.
